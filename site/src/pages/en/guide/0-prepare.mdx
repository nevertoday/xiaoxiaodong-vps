## What this step is

After reading this page you'll know what to prepare, roughly what it costs and how long it all takes. No hands-on work yet.

## Three words to know first

You only need these three to begin; you'll pick up the rest as you go.

- **Server** (<Term k="VPS" />): a computer you rent in a data center abroad, running 24/7. You never see it; you control it remotely.
- **<Term k="节点" text="Node" />**: the encrypted tunnel from your computer to that server. Websites see the server's IP, not yours.
- **<Term k="订阅" text="Subscription" />**: a URL. Your app opens it to get the nodes and the rules for "which sites go through the server", and keeps them updated.

## First: your computer and the server

The whole guide involves just two machines, and the split is simple:

| | What it is | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Your computer** | The Windows or Mac you use every day | Install Codex and talk to it; at the end, install the apps you browse with |
| **The server** | The computer you rent in a data center abroad | Buy it and note down its IP and password. That's all |

**You don't install anything on the server, and you don't log in to it.** The AI on your computer sets everything up there remotely.

## Roughly what it costs

- **Server**: the only required cost, paid quarterly or yearly; check the provider's site for prices. 1 vCPU and 1 GB RAM are enough.
- **Computer app**: Clash Verge Rev, free.
- **iPhone app**: Shadowrocket, a one-off purchase of about US$3 on the US App Store (check the store page).
- **Codex** (optional): needs a paid ChatGPT plan. Skip the AI route and you skip this cost.
- **Certificate, rules, AI API software**: all free. No domain needed.

## How long it takes

About an hour the first time, mostly buying the server and installing apps. Letting AI configure the server takes around 10 minutes. A second server is much faster.

<Callout type="warn" title="Finish before you leave">
Many tools are hard to reach where the internet is restricted — AI tools, app download sites, sometimes even GitHub. Do the whole process before you travel, and test on both phone and computer.
</Callout>

## Two routes — pick one now

- **Let AI do it (recommended)**: in step 1 you install Codex on your computer. In step 3 you paste one message to it and it does the rest.
- **Type it yourself**: you don't have a ChatGPT plan. Step 3 lists every command — copy, paste, Enter, in your own computer's terminal.

Chat-only AI (chat in a web page or a phone app) can't do it for you: it can't run commands, so it just walks you through typing them one by one, which is the manual route anyway.

Both routes give exactly the same result; only who does step 3 differs.

<Callout type="no">
Never post your server's IP, passwords or subscription URL in group chats or screenshots. They're like house keys — whoever has them can use your line.
</Callout>

## Stuck?

<Q q="I know nothing about command lines. Can I still do this?">
Yes. On the AI route you copy one message and answer a few questions. On the manual route you copy commands, paste them and press Enter, and every step tells you what you should see.
</Q>
<Q q="How many people can use one server?">
You and your family, on many devices at once. Don't share it with outsiders: your traffic runs out, what they do is attributed to your server, and the IP may get blocked.
</Q>
<Q q="Why not just buy a ready-made “airport” service?">
Airports share a pool of servers among many people — cheap and easy. Your own server is used only by you, so strangers can't ruin its IP, and you get your own AI API too. Both are valid; this guide covers the second.
</Q>
